Comparing the accountsAI draft

Where did it happen?

Luke: Jesus “withdrew to a town called Bethsaida” (9:10). Mark: “a solitary place” (6:32), after which the disciples sail “on ahead of Him to Bethsaida” (6:45). John: “the other side of the Sea of Galilee” (6:1).

Proposed explanations: a short crossing along the shore after the meal; a second place called Bethsaida; or the writers arranging the scene differently. Citations to be added in review.

Culture & customAI draft

Barley loaves

Only John says the loaves were barley (6:9, 6:13). Barley was the cheaper grain: “A quart of wheat for a denarius, and three quarts of barley for a denarius” (Rev 6:6).

2 · The light follows the text

Eight light states, each tied to the words that trigger it

The scene stays neutral unless a gospel states or clearly implies a time. Pick a state to light the whole page with it, or play the final week, where the accounts are full of time cues.

The final week, told by light alone

Eleven moments from the accounts, in order. Play it and the page moves through evening, night, morning, the darkness from the sixth to the ninth hour, evening, the Sabbath, and then the first day of the week.

Steps 10 and 11 are the same morning in two accounts: John says it was still dark, Mark says just after sunrise. In the explorer, the light follows the gospel you're reading.

    4 · The four accounts

    Colours from Roman-era pigments and dyes

    Cinnabar, murex purple, malachite and Egyptian blue. Checked with a colour-vision simulation for the two common kinds of colour blindness: every pair stays distinct, in day and night states, and every colour keeps 3:1 contrast on all eight backgrounds. Lanes also carry a monogram, so colour is never the only cue.

    8 · People in the art

    Two approaches, one scene

    Both draw only what the text describes: green grass (Mk 6:39), groups of hundreds and fifties (Mk 6:40), five loaves and two fish, twelve basketfuls (Mk 6:43), boats on the shore. In both, Jesus is the light, not a figure.
