# Jesus Tracker

A reader for the life and ministry of Jesus: the full text of the four gospels side by side, aligned where they narrate the same event, with an Artemis-style timeline scrubber and a map panel. Audience: churches and small groups, Christians wanting depth, curious sceptics. Plan and the 19 Oct 2026 direction change: `docs/plan.md` (the direction-change section wins). Open requests: `docs/backlog.md`.

## Non-negotiables

- **Objective tool, not commentary.** Show the BSB text; titles describe what the texts narrate, never historical verdicts; no devotional commentary.
- **Every judgement call is visible**: harmony order (sections carry Robertson's §n; cells flag where the harmony departs from a gospel's own order), site identifications (default = highest OpenBible score, competitors listed on the card with their scores out of 1000 and a note saying what the score is; map labels show names only), places that differ by gospel, textual variants (Mk 16:9–20, Jn 7:53–8:11, verses absent from the BSB main text).
- **Don't invent data.** Time-of-day tints only where the text states a time or the inference is widely agreed. Places marked "implied" when not named.
- **No artwork for now** (the user will source imagery or public-domain art separately). Jesus is never drawn; on the map he is a pure white circle with no outline and a faint glow that lies under every other marker and label (Settings offers a white cross, J or crown to compare). His route is a fine white line with no casing or shadow: the newest leg fades from half strength where it starts to full at him, earlier legs stay at half, with an arrow halfway along each recent leg. A place's dot is dark until he has been there, then light. His light on the land (Settings › Light on the land, on by default): the contour lines around him drawn in white, fading with distance; in an area (region or likely area) it grows to cover the area in place of a shaded circle; in mountain sections the candidate peaks are lit too, each joined to his light by a lit corridor: a mountain the text names (the Mount of Olives) alone, the candidates given for one it doesn't name (meta `mountainPeaks`; the transfiguration: Hermon, Meron or Tabor), or else the named peaks within 20 km. At the Sea of Galilee itself its shore lights up all round; where the accounts name different places (Gadara, Gerasa) each gets a pool of light 12 km across. A place the text on screen names keeps its label in view (top priority, shown even zoomed out; labels move to the other side of their dot before they're hidden). Contours are only ever white (no grey or ink contour lines). Finer lines show only where they sit far enough apart on screen, and around Jerusalem and Capernaum finer detail (down to 12.5 m) loads for the close views. Keep it simple and clean: the user found added glows, ripples, trails and a particle map tacky or overworked.
- **The map's route line follows only Jesus**, from meta `mapStart` (Mary visits Elizabeth, §6; nothing before it): sections listed in meta `jesusAbsent` get a plain scene marker and no line; where Jesus is present but no place is named, the map shows a likely area between the places named before and after, never a guessed point. There is always a line to his marker (the user's choice, 20 Oct 2026, replacing "only when he enters"): a named region is a stop (its circle's centre) even on a tour within it, and so is a likely area's centre (`routeStops`); while he drifts there the leg's end follows his marker, and the point he sets off from becomes that stop's joint for the rest of the session. Where the accounts name different places, each gets a dimmer light and counts as visited. Where the text has him making for a place not yet reached (meta `toward`), the map draws a dashed heading part of the way there and the newest leg goes grey. In a scene he isn't in, his marker rests (dimmed, captioned "Jesus") where the text last placed him, and the scene gets an open diamond, never a light. Back at the start (rows before `mapStart` that name no place) the map returns to the standard overview (`HOME`).
- **No modern borders or country names on the map.** The optional Modern places overlay adds today's towns (GeoNames) and the sites' modern names, nothing more.
- The user works by "try, see and test": build options to compare rather than deciding in the abstract. Use Australian English in site copy (quote the BSB verbatim).

## Content rules

- Quote the BSB verbatim — look it up in `data/raw/bsb/bsb.txt`, never from memory. `npm run validate` checks stated times and title quotes against the text.
- Comparison notes (later): "Matthew says…; Mark says…; proposed explanations (harmonising and source-critical), each cited." No verdict. Avoid loaded words: clearly, obviously, contradiction, error, proves, resolves.
- Shoutout cards (parked) need ≥1 citation and a review status (`ai-draft` → `checked` → `expert`); production excludes `ai-draft`.

## Data (all rebuilt by `npm run data`)

- `scripts/build-text.mjs` — BSB → `public/data/text/{MAT,MRK,LUK,JHN,ACT,1CO}.json` (verse text from `bsb.txt`; empty strings for verses the BSB omits) and `{BOOK}.notes.json` (footnotes from the BSB USJ files: `at` = offset of the BSB's mark in the verse, mapped onto `bsb.txt` by non-space characters; `kind` textual / translation / scripture / other; `omits` = omitted verses whose wording the note gives). The reader marks them (`src/reader/Notes.tsx`; Settings › BSB notes).
- `scripts/build-harmony.mjs` — Robertson outline (Gutenberg #36264) → `src/data/harmony.json`; reports coverage (all 3,779 verses covered; 16 verses Robertson splits appear twice).
- `src/data/sections.meta.json` — hand-written: neutral titles, places (`pb: implied`; `wikidata` where OpenBible has none; `from`: where the text has Jesus set out from somewhere other than the last stop, per gospel with a checked quote, e.g. Nazareth in Lk 1:26 and 2:4; `toward`: where it has him making for a place he hasn't reached, e.g. Lk 13:22, same form), `mapStart`, feasts, time of day per gospel (`stated` quotes are checked), `jesusAbsent` sections, `areas` (km radius for regions), `mountains` (sections whose text places Jesus on or near a mountain, with the words per book, checked; figurative uses left out), `peaks` (Wikidata IDs → [contour window km, summit search km]), `mountainPeaks` (per mountain section: `named` when the text names the mountain, `candidates` when it doesn't; checked against `mountains` and `peaks` by validate), `dates.anchors` (decimal years, astronomical numbering, AD 30 and AD 33 reckonings; rows between anchors are interpolated).
- `scripts/build-mentions.mjs` — the places each row's BSB text names, per gospel → `src/data/mentions.json` (names as written, longest first and masked, so "Sea of Galilee" isn't also Galilee; aliases such as Gadarenes, Dalmanutha, Olivet in its `WRITTEN` table). The map keeps those labels in view.
- `scripts/build-places.mjs` — OpenBible.info geocoding (CC BY 4.0) → `public/data/places.json` with competing candidates and scores, plus each place's English Wikipedia article via Wikidata, and a photo: OpenBible's for the leading identification, or one hand-picked in meta (`photo: [Commons file, caption]`, used where OpenBible has none or a poor one; captions say only what's pictured). `scripts/lib/commons.mjs` fetches author and licence from Wikimedia Commons and a 500 px thumbnail (cached in `data/raw/commons/`) → `public/data/photos/{key}.jpg` (~2.5 MB).
- `scripts/build-geo.mjs` — Natural Earth land/lakes/rivers, Italy to Persia (full detail in the gospel region, simplified with distance; reservoirs, canals and 20th-century lake shapes left out) → `public/data/geo/*.json`.
- `scripts/build-modern.mjs` — GeoNames towns over 15,000 (CC BY 4.0) → `public/data/geo/modern.json` for the overlay.
- `scripts/build-terrain.mjs` — peaks from Wikidata (CC0) and AWS Terrain Tiles at zoom 11 (SRTM/GMTED2010, ETOPO1) → `public/data/peaks.json` (summit elevations) and `public/data/terrain/{cluster}.json` (smoothed contours, loaded per mountain section); also `public/data/terrain/region.json` for the light on the land (about 1.5 MB: contours traced the same way over Phoenicia to Judea, classes every 200 / 100 / 50 m with 50 m only within 20 km of Jerusalem, and Egypt at 100 m from zoom-8 tiles) and `detail-{jerusalem,capernaum}.json` (12.5 m levels from zoom-12 tiles within 9 km, loaded only as Jesus nears them). It also traces the Sea of Galilee's shore from zoom-12 tiles (1 m above the water level) and puts it in place of Natural Earth's in `public/data/geo/lakes.json`, so the lake lines up with the contours. Each point carries a slope code (0.2 % × 2^(q/3)) so the map can show a class only where its lines sit far enough apart; integer mercator coordinates and codes, delta-encoded. Takes about 2 minutes. `scripts/lib/wikidata.mjs` caches Wikidata lookups.
- Raw downloads in `data/raw/` (git-ignored; terrain tiles and Wikidata are cached, so rebuilds work offline); URLs + SHA-256 in `data/SOURCES.md`.

## Architecture

- Astro 7 static site, one page (`src/pages/index.astro`) mounting the React reader with `client:only`. Assets go to `assets/` (not `_astro`, which the Artifact host reserves).
- `src/reader/`: `model.ts` (rows, order flags, repeats, light, `jesus`, `mountain`, dates), `state.ts` (zustand UI store incl. `map`/`mapShare`/`spacing`/`reckoning`/`compress`/`traveller`/`wander`/`landLight`/`modern` + vanilla `playhead`/`layout` stores + `nav` commands), `layoutModel.ts` (even / chronological / verse-count layouts, `rowAtU`), `Synopsis.tsx` (aligned rows, measuring, two-way scroll sync), `Timeline.tsx` (canvas scrubber, year ruler), `MapPane.tsx` (MapLibre 6, lazy-loaded; worker bundled via `?worker&url` + `setWorkerUrl`; `maxBounds` keeps the view on the data; route line (fine white lines, no casing: newest leg 50% → 100% white, earlier legs 50%, arrows halfway), areas, peaks and contours in mountain sections, Modern places overlay, place popups (hover: name, links and story count; click/tap pins the full card: photo with credit, the stories set there as links via `nav.showRow`, which opens a gospel that has the passage; meanwhile the timeline pins those stories via the `placeMarks` store) and card links; framing keeps clear of the map card), `traveller.ts` (Jesus' marker, a white circle: keeps pace with the camera flight, the newest leg's `line-gradient` grows with it; drifts within an area, set frame by frame so the drift grows from rest on arrival and a journey sets off from wherever the drift has taken it, never via the centre; rests dimmed in scenes he isn't in, travelling there at its own pace (`setOff`); its glow is a second marker kept in step and placed first after the canvas; reports where it's drawn (`onDraw`), where it sets off from (`onDepart`) and arrival (`onArrive`), which MapPane uses to keep the route's newest leg and any heading joined to it (redrawn at most every 40 ms) and to move the light; reduced motion jumps), `landLight.ts` (the light on the land: a MapLibre custom WebGL2 layer above the veil and under the route; instanced line segments with each vertex stored once, the region's lines cut into pieces filed in a grid of ~8 km cells so a frame draws only the rows the light reaches; a pool of `POINT_PX` about the marker that blends into an area's centre and radius when it covers one; in a mountain section each candidate peak's window is lit and joined to the pool by a tapered corridor, and the regional lines step aside inside the windows, whose own contours it draws in white (the map has no ink contour layer); detail zones load within 30 km and fade in, the regional lines stepping aside inside them; classes come in as the map zooms in (100 m lines only from zoom 9.4, so a region's broad view reads as 200 m lines) and the vertex shader hides a class where its lines would sit under ~3 px apart, from the stored slope; the lake's shore lights when the scene is the lake (`setOutline`) and each place the accounts differ on gets a pool (`setPools`); the mountain effect takes `nearPeaks` memoised on the peak IDs, so moving between sections on one mountain doesn't tear its peaks down (they flickered); declare `precision highp int` in both shaders, as an int uniform shared by both otherwise fails to link), `mapLabels.ts` (labels placed in priority order, overlapping ones hidden; a place's or peak's label moves to the left of its dot (class `flip`) before it's hidden, and the labels the reader needs (priority under 1: the place shown, places the text names, a heading) are placed together, trying each side for each), `Splitter.tsx` (gospels ↔ map divider), `geo.ts`, `Reader.tsx` (toolbar chips, Go to, settings, tint, map-only navigation), `places.tsx`.
- Map markers are DOM `Marker`s; the toolbar's `.icon` class is global, so don't reuse that class name inside the map. MapLibre writes each marker element's `opacity` inline on every frame, so show/hide or fade an element inside the marker, never the marker element. Markers stack in DOM order: to put one under all the others (a glow), insert its element right after the map's canvas. The light's colours ease in (`--light-duration`, which the minifier writes as `.9s`, so parse its unit), so the map re-reads them once they've settled; guard map colour updates with `getLayer`, not `isStyleLoaded` (false whenever a source is loading tiles). `?debug` exposes the map as `window.__jtMap` for tests.
- Panels: gospel chips and the Map chip; at least one panel stays on. The map takes `mapShare` of the panes (default `min(0.5, 1.5 / (gospels + 1.5))`); the divider clamps to 20–80% and past either limit gives one side the whole screen. With only the map open, the timeline sets the row directly (no reader to scroll).
- Scroll sync: the reader measures row offsets; the playhead is the reading line at 22% of the visible height; timeline position u = row start + fraction × row width in every spacing. Scrubs ease in (long jumps cut to a screen away first) and stop if anything else scrolls. `.reader` has `overflow-anchor: none` so the browser doesn't fight the place-keeping on reflow.
- TypeScript pinned to 6.x (`@astrojs/check` doesn't support 7 yet).

## Commands

- `npm run dev` · `npm run build` · `npm run check` · `npm test` (Playwright on the installed Edge, desktop + Pixel 7; needs `npm run build` first)
- Several sessions at once on this checkout: the shared config reuses one server on port 4321 (parallel runs stop each other's), and Astro allows only one `astro preview` per project. To test alongside others, copy `dist/` to your job folder, serve it with a small static server on a port of your own, and point a private Playwright config (extending `playwright.config.ts`, with its own `outputDir`) at it. Check in with the other sessions before exporting or publishing the review Artifact, since an export ships everyone's work.
- `npm run data` · `npm run validate`
- `npm run export:artifact` → `dist-artifact/` for the review link; smoke-test it with `node scripts/serve-artifact.mjs` and `$env:ARTIFACT=1; npx playwright test tests/artifact.spec.ts`, then publish `dist-artifact/index.html` with the files listed in `dist-artifact/files.json` (root `dist-artifact`), keeping the same Artifact URL.
