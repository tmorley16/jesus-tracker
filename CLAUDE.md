# Jesus Tracker

A reader for the life and ministry of Jesus: the full text of the four gospels side by side, aligned where they narrate the same event, with an Artemis-style timeline scrubber and a map panel. Audience: churches and small groups, Christians wanting depth, curious sceptics. Plan and the 19 Oct 2026 direction change: `docs/plan.md` (the direction-change section wins). Open requests: `docs/backlog.md`.

## Non-negotiables

- **Objective tool, not commentary.** Show the BSB text; titles describe what the texts narrate, never historical verdicts; no devotional commentary.
- **Every judgement call is visible**: harmony order (sections carry Robertson's §n; cells flag where the harmony departs from a gospel's own order), site identifications (default = highest OpenBible score, competitors listed on the card with their scores, a hover tooltip saying what the score is (out of 1000) to keep the card compact; map labels show names only), places that differ by gospel, textual variants (Mk 16:9–20, Jn 7:53–8:11, verses absent from the BSB main text).
- **Don't invent data.** Time-of-day tints only where the text states a time or the inference is widely agreed (the user asked for a scene to keep the light of the text either side of it in one continuous story, e.g. the Last Supper as night after Jn 13:30, Mt 28:2–4 as dawn). The light changes within a passage where the text does (Mk 1:35 dark → daybreak → day as the reading line passes the verses). From Jesus' death (Mt 27:50 and parallels) until the angel at the tomb (Mt 28:2) his glow and his light on the land go out and Dynamic colour turns cold (tint `dead`). Places marked "implied" when not named.
- **No artwork for now** (the user will source imagery or public-domain art separately). Jesus is never drawn; on the map he is a white circle with a fine gold ring and a faint glow that lies under every other marker and label (since 21 Oct 2026, the only marker: a plain white dot was lost among the lit contours and the visited places' dots; a cross, J and crown were tried and dropped). His route is a fine white line with no casing or shadow: the newest leg fades from half strength where it starts to full at him, earlier legs stay at half, with an arrow halfway along each recent leg. A place's dot is dark until he has been there, then plain white with no outline (so it reads apart from his ringed dot). His light on the land (map layers › Effects, on by default): the contour lines around him drawn in white, fading with distance; in an area (region or likely area) it grows to cover the area in place of a shaded circle; in mountain sections the candidate peaks are lit too, each joined to his light by a lit corridor: a mountain the text names (the Mount of Olives) alone, the candidates given for one it doesn't name (meta `mountainPeaks`; the transfiguration: Hermon, Meron or Tabor), or else the named peaks within 20 km. At the Sea of Galilee itself its shore lights up all round; where the accounts name different places (Gadara, Gerasa) each gets a pool of light 12 km across. A place the text on screen names keeps its label in view (top priority, shown even zoomed out; labels move to the other side of their dot before they're hidden). Contours are only ever white (no grey or ink contour lines). Finer lines show only where they sit far enough apart on screen, and around Jerusalem and Capernaum finer detail (down to 12.5 m) loads for the close views. Keep it simple and clean: the user found added glows, ripples, trails and a particle map tacky or overworked.
- **The map's route line follows only Jesus**, from meta `mapStart` (Mary visits Elizabeth, §6; nothing before it): sections listed in meta `jesusAbsent` get a plain scene marker and no line; where Jesus is present but no place is named, the map shows a likely area between the places named before and after (no wider however many sections pass unnamed), never a guessed point. There is always a line from his marker back to the last named place (the user's choice, 20 Oct 2026). An area (a region; where the accounts name different places, the area between them; or a likely area) is a stop, visited at one point where his marker waits, not at its centre (`route.ts`): on the straight way between the named places either side when that passes within 60% of its radius of the centre (moved along the way, never back past the place it's going to, to keep 30% of the radius from the places either side), otherwise that far in, towards the way, and kept at least 30% of the radius from the named places either side so the visit shows; on a trip out and back to one place, and between places the accounts differ on, its centre; and always on dry land, the drift's reach from any shore (the user's notes, 20 Oct 2026: the marker had waited over the Sea of Galilee and by the Dead Sea). The route runs in straight lines and turns at an area's point through a small circular arc whose middle is the point itself, so the line always reaches where he waits (a fillet: a quarter of the area's radius, at most 25 km, tighter at a sharp turn so the corner it rounds lies within a fifth of the radius of the point), the user's choice on 20 Oct 2026 after trying curves (balloon shapes felt unreal), straight on between named places (lost the region visits) and corners at the centres (zigzags in empty space). Named places keep their corners; out and back along the same way stays a straight line. The route is planned once, whole, and every leg is a stretch of that one line: the newest leg (from the last named place he left to where he is, growing from where he sets off) is rounded from the start and stays as it is once he moves on, and it always leaves an area from where he waits, never from where his drift took him (the user's notes, 20 Oct 2026). His marker drifts a little about where it waits (12% of the area's radius, at most 14 px); setting off, the drift dies away as he travels along the line. Where the accounts name different places, each gets a dimmer light and counts as visited, and his marker waits between them. Where the text has him making for a place not yet reached (meta `toward`), the map draws a dashed heading part of the way there and the newest leg goes grey. In a scene he isn't in, his marker rests (dimmed, captioned "Jesus") where the text last placed him, and the scene gets an open diamond, never a light; from his birth (meta `birth`) the resting marker keeps a little of its glow and of his light on the land (the user's wish: glowing a bit while alive), out from his death until the angel at the tomb (meta `death`). Back at the start (rows before `mapStart` that name no place) the map returns to the standard overview (`HOME`).
- **No modern borders or country names on the map.** The optional Modern places overlay adds today's towns (GeoNames) and the sites' modern names, nothing more.
- The user works by "try, see and test": build options to compare rather than deciding in the abstract. Use Australian English in site copy (quote the BSB verbatim).

## Content rules

- Quote the BSB verbatim — look it up in `data/raw/bsb/bsb.txt`, never from memory. `npm run validate` checks stated times and title quotes against the text.
- Comparison notes (later): "Matthew says…; Mark says…; proposed explanations (harmonising and source-critical), each cited." No verdict. Avoid loaded words: clearly, obviously, contradiction, error, proves, resolves.
- Shoutout cards (parked) need ≥1 citation and a review status (`ai-draft` → `checked` → `expert`); production excludes `ai-draft`.

## Data (all rebuilt by `npm run data`)

- `scripts/build-text.mjs` — BSB → `public/data/text/{MAT,MRK,LUK,JHN,ACT,1CO}.json` (verse text from `bsb.txt`; empty strings for verses the BSB omits) and `{BOOK}.notes.json` (footnotes from the BSB USJ files: `at` = offset of the BSB's mark in the verse, mapped onto `bsb.txt` by non-space characters; `kind` textual / translation / scripture / other; `omits` = omitted verses whose wording the note gives). The reader marks them (`src/reader/Notes.tsx`; Gospel settings › BSB notes).
- `scripts/build-harmony.mjs` — Robertson outline (Gutenberg #36264) → `src/data/harmony.json`; reports coverage (all 3,779 verses covered; 16 verses Robertson splits appear twice).
- `src/data/sections.meta.json` — hand-written: neutral titles, places (`pb: implied`; `wikidata` where OpenBible has none; `from`: where the text has Jesus set out from somewhere other than the last stop, per gospel with a checked quote, e.g. Nazareth in Lk 1:26 and 2:4; `toward`: where it has him making for a place he hasn't reached, e.g. Lk 13:22, same form), `mapStart`, `birth` (from this section his resting marker keeps a little glow), feasts, time of day per gospel (`stated` quotes are checked; a gospel may give several states in order, each later one with an `at` verse where it takes effect; the state that took effect latest wins, then the most common), `death` (the verse of Jesus' death per gospel and the section and verse it ends at, checked), `jesusAbsent` sections, `areas` (km radius for regions; a region's position is in `places`: the wilderness sits between Bethany and Jericho, clear of the Dead Sea, and Beyond the Jordan is Perea, east of the river, by the user's notes), `mountains` (sections whose text places Jesus on or near a mountain, with the words per book, checked; figurative uses left out), `peaks` (Wikidata IDs → [contour window km, summit search km]), `mountainPeaks` (per mountain section: `named` when the text names the mountain, `candidates` when it doesn't; checked against `mountains` and `peaks` by validate), `moves` (sections shown after another than in Robertson's order, each with a note shown under its heading and a checked quote; the anointing at Bethany follows John's date; a moved row doesn't advance the order-flag walk, so only it is flagged), `dates.anchors` (decimal years, astronomical numbering, AD 30 and AD 33 reckonings; rows between anchors are interpolated).
- `src/data/parallels.json` — hand-written sets of passages synopses print side by side that Robertson places on different occasions (first passage ↔ each of the others); the reader shows a "≈ Mt 6:9–13" chip at each passage's first verse that jumps there (`showVerse` in `Notes.tsx`). Validate checks the verses exist and sit in different rows.
- `scripts/build-mentions.mjs` — the places each row's BSB text names, per gospel → `src/data/mentions.json` (names as written, longest first and masked, so "Sea of Galilee" isn't also Galilee; aliases such as Gadarenes, Dalmanutha, Olivet in its `WRITTEN` table). The map keeps those labels in view.
- `scripts/build-places.mjs` — OpenBible.info geocoding (CC BY 4.0) → `public/data/places.json` with competing candidates and scores, plus each place's English Wikipedia article via Wikidata, and a photo: OpenBible's for the leading identification, or one hand-picked in meta (`photo: [Commons file, caption]`, used where OpenBible has none or a poor one; captions say only what's pictured). `scripts/lib/commons.mjs` fetches author and licence from Wikimedia Commons and a 500 px thumbnail (cached in `data/raw/commons/`) → `public/data/photos/{key}.jpg` (~2.5 MB).
- `scripts/build-geo.mjs` — Natural Earth land/lakes/rivers, Italy to Persia (full detail in the gospel region, simplified with distance; reservoirs, canals and 20th-century lake shapes left out) → `public/data/geo/*.json`.
- `scripts/build-modern.mjs` — GeoNames towns over 15,000 (CC BY 4.0) → `public/data/geo/modern.json` for the overlay.
- `scripts/build-terrain.mjs` — peaks from Wikidata (CC0) and AWS Terrain Tiles at zoom 11 (SRTM/GMTED2010, ETOPO1) → `public/data/peaks.json` (summit elevations) and `public/data/terrain/{cluster}.json` (smoothed contours, loaded per mountain section); also `public/data/terrain/region.json` for the light on the land (about 1.5 MB: contours traced the same way over Phoenicia to Judea, classes every 200 / 100 / 50 m with 50 m only within 20 km of Jerusalem, and Egypt at 100 m from zoom-8 tiles) and `detail-{jerusalem,capernaum}.json` (12.5 m levels from zoom-12 tiles within 9 km, loaded only as Jesus nears them). It also traces the Sea of Galilee's shore from zoom-12 tiles (the cells within 1 m of the water level, opened and closed with a ~100 m disc to drop inlets and spits, blurred ~80 m, about 225 points: the raw trace lit up as a blotchy band) and puts it in place of Natural Earth's in `public/data/geo/lakes.json`, so the lake lines up with the contours. Each point carries a slope code (0.2 % × 2^(q/3)) so the map can show a class only where its lines sit far enough apart; integer mercator coordinates and codes, delta-encoded. Takes about 2 minutes. `scripts/lib/wikidata.mjs` caches Wikidata lookups.
- `scripts/build-peak-photos.mjs` — each peak's Wikidata image (P18; `lib/wikidata.mjs` now caches it) as a Commons thumbnail in `public/data/peak-photos/{id}.jpg` (its own folder, since build-places replaces `photos/`), with its credit added to `peaks.json`; runs after build-terrain. A peak clicked shows in the place card with its photo, height and links.
- `scripts/build-relief.mjs` — the land coloured by height for map layers › Terrain (on by default): zoom-8 terrain tiles about the places in the accounts (shared tile cache and decoder in `scripts/lib/terrain.mjs`), sage low ground → sand → ochre → terracotta → pale stone on Hermon (height only, no land use), the sea flooded clear from the edges (so the rift below sea level keeps its colour), fading into the plain land from 40 to 140 km from the nearest place, the distance wobbled by smooth noise so it has no edge or shape of its own (the user found a box with flat sides jarring) → `public/data/relief.png` (~0.9 MB) and its corners in `src/data/relief.json`; the map lays it over the land fill at 85% (`RELIEF_OPACITY`) and turns the water bluer (`.map-box[data-land]`). Tried 21 Oct 2026 after the user admired thegospelvisualized.org's coloured basemap.
- Raw downloads in `data/raw/` (git-ignored; terrain tiles and Wikidata are cached, so rebuilds work offline); URLs + SHA-256 in `data/SOURCES.md`.

- Map markers are DOM `Marker`s; the toolbar's `.icon` class is global, so don't reuse that class name inside the map. MapLibre writes each marker element's `opacity` inline on every frame, so show/hide or fade an element inside the marker, never the marker element. Markers stack in DOM order: to put one under all the others (a glow), insert its element right after the map's canvas. The light's colours ease in (`--light-duration`, which the minifier writes as `.9s`, so parse its unit), so the map re-reads them once they've settled; guard map colour updates with `getLayer`, not `isStyleLoaded` (false whenever a source is loading tiles). `?debug` exposes the map as `window.__jtMap`, and the route's stops, rest points, planned line and each stop's place on it as `window.__jtRoute`, for tests.
- Panels: gospel chips and the Map chip; at least one panel stays on. The map takes `mapShare` of the panes (default on desktop 1/(1+φ) ≈ 38%, gospels to map in the golden ratio (the user's choice, 21 Oct 2026); on phones, where they stack, `min(0.5, 1.5 / (gospels + 1.5))`); the divider clamps to 20–80% and past either limit gives one side the whole screen. With only the map open, the timeline sets the row directly (no reader to scroll).
- The reader opens at the feeding of the five thousand (`START_ROW` in `model.ts`, found from Mk 6:35), which all four gospels tell, with its heading at the top.
- Scroll sync: the reader measures row offsets; the playhead is the reading line at 22% of the visible height; timeline position u = row start + fraction × row width in every spacing. Scrubs ease in (long jumps cut to a screen away first) and stop if anything else scrolls. `.reader` has `overflow-anchor: none` so the browser doesn't fight the place-keeping on reflow.
- TypeScript pinned to 6.x (`@astrojs/check` doesn't support 7 yet).

## Windows notes

- Shell is PowerShell 5.1. In Claude's shell, refresh PATH first if `node`/`git` aren't found: `$env:Path = [Environment]::GetEnvironmentVariable('Path','Machine') + ';' + [Environment]::GetEnvironmentVariable('Path','User')`.
- Use `npm.cmd`/`npx.cmd` in PowerShell scripts. Line endings are LF (`.gitattributes`). Git commits as `tmorley16` with GitHub's noreply address (repo-local config); `gh` is installed at `C:\Program Files\GitHub CLI\gh.exe` and signed in as tmorley16.
- `.claude/settings.json` sets `worktree.bgIsolation: none` (the user's choice), so background Claude jobs edit this checkout in place. Several may be working at once: check in with them (and let them finish) before committing or pushing, since a commit ships everyone's work.

## Hosting

- Public repo `tmorley16/jesus-tracker`; every push to `main` deploys to GitHub Pages at https://tmorley16.github.io/jesus-tracker/ via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`. The workflow sets `SITE_URL` and `BASE_PATH=/jesus-tracker/` (read in `astro.config.mjs`); local builds and the Artifact export stay at `/`. Built data is committed, so Pages only runs `npm run build`: commit the outputs of `npm run data` along with any script that changes them.
